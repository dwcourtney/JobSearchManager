# Decision: KEEP REGEX FOR NOW

The task-specific encoders do not meet the combined recall and workload-reduction objective under employer-disjoint evaluation. This experiment does not justify a production replacement or a claim that training on the current provisional labels solved cheap triage.

The one validation-selected candidate reaching 98% evaluation KEEP recall is DeBERTa with body text: 3/150 false rejects, 28/347 evaluation rejects (8.07%), and 98/1503 cache rejects (6.52%). The rules reject 208/1503 (13.84%) with no known false rejects among the reviewed KEEP. No validation-selected trained candidate reaches 99% evaluation recall. The 98%, 99% and zero-miss validation targets select identical thresholds because each validation fold has only 11–15 KEEP examples.

The higher-volume settings are unsafe: DeBERTa title-only rejects 20.16% of cache but loses 10 KEEP; BERT-Tiny body rejects 23.29% but loses 12. Errors include Kahua Application Developer, enterprise systems engineering, PC Support Technician, Information System Security Officer and cybersecurity management. These are not merely disagreements over marginal unrelated occupations. The known General Finance correction was applied before training; it is still falsely rejected by BERT-Tiny body when evaluated out of employer.

Title-only is not sufficient at the required recall. Adding body improves DeBERTa recall from 93.33% to 98.00% while more than halving rejection volume. For BERT-Tiny, body text increases rejection but reduces recall from 96.67% to 92.00%. Body context is not a consistent improvement with this dataset and truncation scheme.

Generalization is uneven. In the employer fold containing Amentum, Leidos, NXP, Parsons and RTX, title DeBERTa retains only 82% of KEEP and body BERT-Tiny only 76%, despite perfect validation recall. On low-similarity titles, body BERT-Tiny retains only 23/28 KEEP (82.14%). Body DeBERTa retains all 28 but rejects only three of the 76 novel-title rows. The model either keeps nearly everything or makes unsafe occupational decisions depending on input and employer shift. Broad title-family-disjoint generalization remains unproven; exact-title isolation and a lexical-novelty slice cannot substitute for it.

Literal employer-name masking changes 26/347 decisions for BERT-Tiny body, increasing false rejects to 17. Body-tail substitution changes 149/347 decisions and causes 70 KEEP misses. This is strong input-position/context sensitivity, consistent with an unreliable use of employer/boilerplate context, though tail substitution is an out-of-distribution test and cannot identify one causal phrase. DeBERTa body has six company-mask flips and unchanged aggregate false-reject count. DeBERTa title-only casefolding changes 57/347 decisions and raises misses from 10 to 24. An earlier combined casefold/masking probe would have wrongly attributed many of those changes to employer names; the separate case-preserving control avoids that interpretation. Products/subsidiaries and unnamed boilerplate remain unmasked, so low masking sensitivity is not proof of absence of shortcuts.

The thresholds are not calibrated confidence. Validation-selected boundaries differ substantially by fold, and perfect validation KEEP recall does not transfer. Retrospective global and independently optimized per-fold thresholds are reported only as diagnostics. Even the per-fold oracle, which gets to use evaluation labels and cache counts, reaches at most 13.57% cache rejection at 98% recall and 10.84% at >=99%. The close 13.57% versus 13.84% volume difference is not meaningful statistically, but known KEEP losses and failure to choose safe thresholds without evaluation labels are meaningful. Do not deploy oracle thresholds.

The unreviewed-cache subset excludes 347 deliberately reviewed examples and therefore has a different mix: rules reject only 46/1156 (3.98%), while DeBERTa body rejects 70/1156 (6.06%). That difference is not proof of better safe rejection: these 70 decisions lack labels, and the model already misses viable jobs in reviewed evaluation. Use the requested full-cache baseline for the primary volume comparison; neither cache count measures true-reject precision.

BERT-Tiny inference is practical: approximately 0.30 ms/title batched on GPU, 2.79 ms with body; CPU single-posting medians approximately 1.23 ms and 5.93 ms. DeBERTa is approximately 1.47 ms/title or 21.26 ms/body batched on GPU. One bounded encoder pass avoids generative inference, but speed is not the blocker here. Clean-process GPU/CPU memory and latency distributions are in the detailed report. Training the twelve fixed runs took about 16.5 minutes total in measured training sections, including validation/checkpoint saves; downloads, diagnostics and setup are additional.

If further work is authorized, improve the labels and coverage before adding model complexity: human-adjudicate the existing 347 rows, collect roughly 1,000–2,000 additional development examples across substantially more employers and adjacent technical/negative occupation families, and freeze a separate evaluation sample with at least 300 confirmed KEEP plus several hundred REJECT. With zero misses on a fixed classifier and independent sample, 299 KEEP are required for a one-sided 95% lower recall bound of 99%; observed misses, correlated employers and repeated selection demand more. The development-label budget is a planning estimate, not a measured learning curve or guarantee that more data will fix these models.

No production activation, new rules, generative labeling/inference, production-holdout tuning, commits, or second-stage work. Keep the existing electrical-safe baseline in its current shadow/audit role.
